# Teacher answer key — 2026-09-10 English B Paper 2 Reading

This is a teacher-authored marking guide for a classroom adaptation based on two public articles. It is not an official IB markscheme. Award one mark for each valid point unless a question's mark value indicates otherwise. Accept equivalent wording.

## Text A — Campus gardens

| Question | Answer / marking points | Marks |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | Climate change, health, fairness (any two) | 2 |
| 2 | They involve different combinations of participants. | 1 |
| 3 | Growing vegetables, collecting rainwater, or composting food scraps. | 1 |
| 4 | Students believe waste has no consequences once removed. | 1 |
| 5 | Growing food prompts questions about unequal access to fresh produce / food security. | 2 |
| 6 | Food security and unequal access. | 1 |
| 7 | Any two: connects work to curriculum/community; measures compost; interviews growers; compares food prices/availability; shares learning with families or community groups. | 2 |
| 8 | Projects may lose support when key people leave. | 1 |
| 9 | The garden makes a large abstract issue observable and discussable through a nearby practical example. | 2 |
| 10 | They cannot by themselves replace wider climate changes. | 1 |
| 11 | Students ask better questions, recognise unfair patterns, or join community projects. | 1 |
| 12 | Award up to five: two clearly explained ways, supported by evidence, such as making food systems visible; linking food to fairness/security; developing evidence-based investigation; building collaboration/community action; encouraging questions beyond the garden; or building confidence for wider change. | 5 |

## Text B — Trees, roofs and urban heat

| Question | Answer / marking points | Marks |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 13 | Shade/trees and cooler surfaces in one street versus little shade/heat-retaining asphalt in another. | 2 |
| 14 | Trees and cool roofs. | 1 |
| 15 | Supports wildlife or makes walking/public space more comfortable. | 1 |
| 16 | It needs time and continuing care to provide benefits. | 1 |
| 17 | Any two: trees need years to grow; young trees need watering/maintenance; roots need space; planting can conflict with pipes, power lines or narrow pavements. | 2 |
| 18 | They can sometimes be installed more quickly and reduce indoor heat. | 1 |
| 19 | Roofs do not shade people outdoors, such as people waiting at bus stops; outdoor heat exposure can remain. | 2 |
| 20 | Heat and vulnerability. | 1 |
| 21 | To illustrate the possible reach of a combined strategy. | 1 |
| 22 | Where pavements become unbearable, which bus stops lack shelter, or which buildings stay hot at night (any two). | 2 |
| 23 | Which combination protects the most vulnerable people in this place? | 1 |
| 24 | Award up to five: evidence identifies heat and vulnerability; residents identify local exposure and practical gaps; combining both targets investment; the approach accounts for feasibility/cost; listening builds trust for long-term maintenance/access. | 5 |

**Total: 40 marks.**

### Source notes

Text A is an original classroom adaptation based on Zoë Y. Deskin and Blane Harvey’s study, “Critical food systems education in university student-run food initiatives: learning opportunities for food systems transformation,” reported in “Campus garden initiatives can help grow the next generation of environmental change-makers,” *McGill University Newsroom*, 11 April 2024. Verified link: https://www.mcgill.ca/newsroom/channels/news/campus-garden-initiatives-can-help-grow-next-generation-environmental-change-makers-356720

Text B is an original classroom adaptation based on Ian A. Smith, Dan Li, David K. Fork, Gregory A. Wellenius and Lucy R. Hutyra, “Integrated tree canopy expansion and cool roofs can optimize air temperature and heat exposure reductions in Boston,” *Communications Earth & Environment*, 1 July 2025. Verified link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02462-3. Supporting factual guidance: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, “Using Cool Roofs to Reduce Heat Islands,” https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/using-cool-roofs-reduce-heat-islands
