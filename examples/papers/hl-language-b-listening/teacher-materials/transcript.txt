Hello, I am Mira, and today I am speaking from the repair cafe at Eastbrook Community Library.

The idea began last spring. A group of students noticed that people were throwing away lamps, bags, and small appliances that needed only simple repairs. We asked the library for one table, but the first session filled the whole meeting room. We now meet on the second Saturday of every month.

Our volunteers bring different skills. One is a retired electrical engineer. Members of the local sewing club mend fabric, and a bicycle mechanic helps with wheels, brakes, and chains. The most important lesson they teach is not to rush. First, label every loose part and work out why the object failed.

During our first four sessions, visitors brought sixty-eight objects. We repaired forty-one of them. Twelve more became safe learning samples so that young volunteers could practise taking things apart. We separated the remaining items for recycling.

The numbers are encouraging, but we do not judge success only by how many objects leave working. We ask visitors whether they feel more confident about attempting a repair next time. A repaired toaster is useful; a person who understands why it stopped working may prevent several future objects from being discarded.

Our next plan is a mobile tool cart that can visit neighbourhoods too far from the library. We have enough donated tools, but we still need space in the school minibus and secure storage between visits. If those practical details are solved, the first travelling session could happen before winter.
